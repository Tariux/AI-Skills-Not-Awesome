# Taste Gate — Rationale for No-Slop Bans

This document explains why each banned pattern exists. Understanding the *why* lets you catch tells the list doesn't name.

## Zero-Tolerance Bans

### Em dashes / double hyphens
**Why**: AI models overuse em dashes as a crude "sophistication" signal. Human writers use them sparingly for genuine parenthetical emphasis. Periods, colons, commas, parentheses cover 99% of cases without the tell.

### "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." and cousins
**Why**: The ultimate throat-clearing opener. Signals "I have nothing specific to say, so I'll start with a generic timestamp." Cut to the actual subject.

### "This chapter/article/post explores..."
**Why**: Meta-commentary on the container, not the content. Readers care what you *found*, not what the document *does*.

### "It is important to note..." / "It's worth noting..." / "We're excited to announce..."
**Why**: Performed significance. If it's important, the fact carries weight; the flag weakens it. "We're excited" is marketing, not information.

### "Not X, but Y" cadence and "Not X. It is Y."
**Why**: Synthetic contrast. Real writing contrasts when the opposition is genuine and surprising; AI manufactures foils.

### "That is..." as sentence opener; "By understanding X, readers can..."
**Why**: Pedagogical frame that treats readers as students. Adults don't need "by understanding gravity, you can avoid falling."

### Hedges: "arguably", "in many ways", "to some extent", "one could argue"
**Why**: Evades commitment. If the claim is uncertain, say *why* specifically ("studies disagree on..."). If it's certain, state it.

### Recap markers: "In short", "Ultimately", "At the end of the day", "In conclusion"
**Why**: Waste the reader's time summarizing what they just read. If the piece needs a recap, it's too long or poorly structured.

### Filler verbs/nouns: `delve`, `leverage` (verb), `unpack`, `seamless`, `game-changer`, `deep dive`, `empower`, `elevate`, `supercharge`, `unleash`
**Why**: Semantic inflation. "Leverage" means "use"; "unpack" means "explain"; "game-changer" means "significant change." Use the precise word.

### Stock flourishes: `a testament to`, `plays a crucial role`, `navigate the complexities`, `in an era of`, `in a world where`, `whether you're a...`, `it's no secret`, `look no further`, `let's dive in`, `treasure trove`, `a beacon of`, `tapestry of`, `in the realm of`
**Why**: Template phrases that could appear in any corporate blog. They carry zero information about your specific subject.

### Performed candor: `let's be honest`, `let's be real`, `let's face it`, `real talk`, `here's the thing`, `here's the kicker`, `here's the catch`
**Why**: Simulated intimacy. Also injection failures — they're prompts masquerading as prose. `let us be clear` is formal register, not this.

### Perfectly symmetrical sections; generic recap after every section
**Why**: Structural AI tell. Real writing has organic rhythm; forced symmetry reads like a slide deck.

### Fake-deep abstractions with no quote or case behind them; strategy-deck phrasing
**Why**: Abstraction without grounding. "The paradigm shift toward holistic integration" — what *actually* changed? Name the system, the metric, the team.

## Narrative Tells (Fiction/Story-Driven)

### Recycled emotional choreography
`breath catching`, `jaw clenching`, `heart hammering` — these are the AI's stock "show don't tell" kit. Real bodies react in specific, weird ways.

### Named emotions instead of action
"she felt a surge of determination" vs. "she reopened the pull request at 2am." The action *is* the evidence.

### Tidy-summary endings
"For the first time, I understood..." — ends on the realization, not the image or action that follows. Life doesn't resolve in the paragraph that raised the tension.

### Sentiment skew
Dark scenes silver-lined, tension resolved in the paragraph that raised it. Real difficulty lingers.

### One arc stamp on every scene
Every scene follows the same emotional trajectory. Real scenes vary: some are static, some regressive, some lateral.

### Cluster metaphors
Weight, light, drowning — the same three metaphors rotating. Expand the imagery or cut it.

### Zero subtext
Everything on the surface. Real communication is 90% what's not said.

## How to Use This Gate

**Write mode**: Before delivering, scan your draft for each category. If a banned pattern appears, it's not "style" — it's a structural tell. Rewrite the *sentence*, not the word.

**Audit mode**: Run Gate 1 (fidelity) first — verify every quote, claim, specific. Then Gate 2 (taste) — scan for the patterns above. Report findings with line references. Flag judgment calls (unverifiable-but-probably-real quotes) for the user.

**The style test**: If a paragraph could appear in a generic business ebook, cut or rewrite it.
**The repetition budget**: Rotate examples, include an instructive failure, never let three sections share one skeleton.
